An international collaboration, the Ancient Panel Painting: Examination, Analysis, and Research (APPEAR) project was developed to collect technical information on ancient panel paintings, specifically funerary mummy portraits produced in Egypt during the Roman era, to build a platform for comparative study. Through the participation of museums from around the world, APPEAR aims to stimulate exploration and shed light on the use and practices of ancient Egyptian panel painting production. This effort, in turn, allows for greater insight into how ancient artists worked and the pivotal contributions they made to the development of painting. As a Getty initiative, APPEAR supports the Museum’s mission of “advancing the understanding and preservation of the visual arts locally and throughout the world,” and complements the Getty Villa’s research, exhibition, and publication program *The Classical World in Context*, which explores the interconnectivity of ancient cultures throughout the Mediterranean and Near East.

APPEAR began small, with the aim of studying the sixteen paintings within the Getty collection. Through the participation of institutions with similar funerary portraits, it has since grown to encompass a third of the known panel paintings—currently residing in more than sixty collections from around the world. The opportunity to study such a large body of material dating from the first through third century CE, reflecting a wide diversity of cultural, artistic, and funerary traditions, is unique.

Thanks to APPEAR’s insights into the materials, sourcing, and methodologies of Romano-Egyptian mummy portraits, great strides have been made in the understanding of this corpus. There is still much to discover, however, and further progress will depend on collaboration between museums and scholars via a participant-led database. Dissemination of this information is made possible through Getty’s support of the database, as well as conferences and publications. The APPEAR project contributes to our knowledge of how people lived, worked, worshipped, and died in ancient Egypt, and it is a model collaboration, providing an example for future partnerships with far-reaching benefits in the study of antiquities.

We are delighted to publish the proceedings of the second APPEAR conference held in 2022, *Mummy Portraits of Roman Egypt, Volume 2: Emerging Research from the APPEAR Project*, online and by print-on-demand. The papers in this publication and the 2020 volume from the first conference, as well as the numerous exhibitions and technical and interpretive investigations that have evolved from the APPEAR project, are a tribute to its success, and more generally to collaborative scholarship on antiquity.
